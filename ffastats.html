What it tracks

Seven numbers, each of which can be switched off if you don't want it on the board:

StatWhy it's there
KillsThe headline number, and the one people compare
DeathsMeaningless alone, essential next to kills
Wins / LossesOutcomes rather than raw fights — a different measure of doing well
Best streakA personal record to beat, which is what keeps people playing
Current streakThe one that creates tension. A long streak makes you play differently
K/D ratioThe number people actually argue about

How it works

Current streak is the number that changes behaviour

Kills and deaths are history — they tell you what already happened and nothing about right now. A current streak is live. It goes up while you're winning and it's gone the moment you're not, which means a player sitting on a good one plays more carefully than they otherwise would.

That's the whole reason both streaks are on the board rather than just a total. Best streak gives you something to chase; current streak gives you something to lose. On an FFA server, where there's no objective other than fighting, those two numbers are most of what makes a session feel like it has stakes.

Everything on the board is a toggle

All seven stats are individually switchable in the config, because the right board depends on the server. A pure deathmatch server might want kills, streaks and K/D and nothing else. A server with rounds cares about wins and losses. Showing everything by default and letting people delete what they don't want is easier than making them assemble it.

The board title and its colours are configurable too — it ships with a purple-to-pink gradient, and both ends are set in the config rather than hardcoded, so it can be made to match a server's existing branding instead of clashing with it.

There's also a seasonal flag, for servers that want to reset the table and start a new competition rather than have month-one players permanently at the top.

Small on purpose

The whole plugin is a few kilobytes. It does one thing — count some numbers and put them on a scoreboard — and it doesn't need a database, a permissions tree or a dependency to do it.

Not everything has to be AegisStaff. A server owner who wants a stats board should be able to drop in a jar, restart, and be finished, and anything that made that harder would be working against the point of it.
